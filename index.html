CONFIDENTIAL — DISCUSSION DRAFT
Spectrum and satellite capacity are to space what real estate is to land. We see an opportunity for Luxembourg to host the trading layer. The industry is growing; capacity is finite. Today deals are bilateral, slow, and expensive. Luxembourg's satellite heritage and digital-asset laws make it a natural venue for this market.
01 — The problem

A market that moves at the speed of paper.

Every capacity deal is a one-off negotiation across multiple parties.

  • Weeks to months to agree and coordinate
  • No shared record of terms or delivery
  • High friction few participants can carry the cost
Fashma · Spectrum Market
Every deal is bespoke, slow, and expensive. Only large players can participate. The market is tiny because the friction is enormous. Regulators lack transparent, auditable records of capacity use. We need to make capacity trade as efficient as trading shares.
02 — Why now

The scarce resource is now contested.

LEO and MEO networks are multiplying demand for flexible capacity.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
The infrastructure is in orbit. The economics are proven. What's missing is a trading layer that matches the pace of the industry. These deals show the scale — and the need for infrastructure that can handle it. These are announced values, not market size.
03 — The system

Three parts.
One continuous workflow.

List, verify, deliver — with one on-chain record
from listing to live service.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Three distinct parts, one integrated workflow. Sellers list, the system verifies eligibility, the buyer purchases, and service is activated. This is not a manual brokerage — it's an automated trading layer.
04 — The instrument

A right to use,
not a title deed.

One service, one non-transferable on-chain card.

PRICED IN SRU

a unit of account.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Think of it like a hotel room: the guest buys a right to use a room for a period. The guest doesn't own the building. Same idea: a right to use capacity for a defined window. SRU is our unit of account — like kWh for electricity. The NFT is a non-transferable handle to the contract, not spectrum title. The allocation reference is optional; its presence is the assurance level.
05 — The ladder

Buy at the rung that fits your business.

From raw frequency rights to a delivered service — four levels of assembly.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Different buyers need different things. A service provider needs a complete link. An operator may only need capacity. Our model supports all four rungs. A provider can buy capacity and resell it as a closed link. This expands the market by letting each participant trade at their level.

07 — Trust

A shared record,
held by no one.

Blockchain is the proof. No custodian.

  • On-chain Contract NFT · signatures · document fingerprints
  • Off-chain Documents · price · identities — encrypted
  • Readers Buyer, seller, satellite, ground, regulator
Fashma · Spectrum Market
When you have multiple parties with conflicting interests, you need a record nobody can alter. Fashma operates the register but holds no licence or capacity. Every action is timestamped. Regulators get transparency without Fashma acting as a trusted intermediary. The blockchain explanation and its limits apply here.
08 — The posture

The State keeps the authority. We add the record.

Fashma never holds a licence, concession, or capacity.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
We're not replacing regulators. We're making the trading activity transparent and auditable so regulators can see exactly what's happening. This is a partnership, not a replacement. Non-custodial architecture means regulators don't have to trust us — they have to trust the record. The digital-asset framework exists but does not currently govern spectrum contracts.
09 — The marketplace

A listing you can check, not just believe.

Buyers compare on terms that carry evidence.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
For sellers: list once, get discovered. For buyers: find what you need, purchase it with confidence. Settlement is recorded against the contract. This is not an order book; there is no price index; confidentiality is a feature — terms are on request.
10 — The check

Four checks before a deal becomes a contract.

Four checks, tested against current records in one pass.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Instead of lawyers spending weeks checking whether a deal is legal, the system tests the deal against current records in one pass. The four checks are jurisdiction, time, location, and interference. This is a design objective; we will measure the time saved in the pilot. What remains a counsel opinion is kept as such.
11 — The delivery

The contract becomes
a live link.

Activation, a provider-signed attestation, and an on-chain receipt.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Two different signatures: Spectra M² signs the frequency assignment; the provider signs the activation attestation. The receipt anchors both. Neither signature proves physical service quality, legal compliance, or that the signal was interference-free. On an envelope-backed card the assignment is checked against the envelope, width, deadline, active assignments, and the joint packability of other reservations. If no block is feasible, the card stays in delivery-requested, the buyer may cancel, and no receipt is issued. On a contract-only card there is no assignment at all.
12 — The path

Prove it in
four moves.

Each phase earns the next.

Fashma · Spectrum Market
Phase 1 is a devnet proof of one transaction, with synthetic data: one contract, one NFT, one reservation, one delivery attestation, one receipt. Phase 2 is a regulated pilot with named partners, counsel, and a sandbox. Phase 3 makes rights and delivery programmable in operation. Phase 4 adds privacy-preserving settlement; the privacy design is future and is not claimed today. Each gate needs evidence before the next phase. Do not imply that any phase is live or approved.
